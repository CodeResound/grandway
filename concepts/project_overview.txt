PROJECT OVERVIEW — Grandway
===========================

This is the project-wide grounding / vision document (CLAUDE.md §36). It holds
the whole concept of the project — the "why" and the "what" — and is the file
the team (and any Claude, backend or frontend) returns to for grounding.

It is a living draft, not a rulebook and not a backlog. It does not override
CLAUDE.md and is never, by itself, authorization to build a module — building
still requires a per-app concept file (concepts/<app_name>.txt) and the
§25/§26 pre-coding gates.

Every app named under "Core domains" should get its own
concepts/<app_name>.txt, created from concepts/_TEMPLATE.txt, before that app
is built.


Vision / Problem
----------------

Grandway is an internal operating system for education consultancies that help
people pursue study opportunities in foreign countries. It manages the
applicant lifecycle from the first enquiry, through lead follow-up and applicant
profiling, to study planning, document preparation, offer tracking, appointments,
and the final result of the applicant's journey.

The problem Grandway solves is fragmentation. Applicant information is often
spread across spreadsheets, paper files, chat messages, personal notes, folders,
and separate document templates. This makes it difficult to know who owns a
lead, what information is complete, which documents exist, what is still
pending, what decisions were made, and who changed a record.

Grandway creates one accountable source of truth for the consultancy. It keeps
the applicant's identity, history, study goals, qualifications, documents,
offers, activities, ownership, and outcomes connected in one place. It also
preserves enough history to explain how a record reached its current state.

Version 1 is an internal staff system. It is intentionally simple in its actor
model: every system user is either an Admin or a Lead Manager.


Target users
------------

Grandway V1 has two system actor types.

Admin

An Admin has full authority across the system. An Admin can access and manage
all users, leads, applicants, journeys, institutions, programs,
offers, documents, files, templates, signatures, checklists, appointments,
notifications, reports, and audit records.

Admins can assign and reassign work, correct records, transfer ownership,
archive and restore information, manage system-wide reference records, and
intervene at any point in an applicant's lifecycle.

Admin authority must always remain accountable. Important Admin actions must be
recorded with the actor, affected record, action, time, previous value, new
value, and reason where appropriate.

Lead Manager

A Lead Manager is the primary operational user. The Lead Manager receives and
manages leads, performs follow-up, converts qualified leads into applicants,
maintains applicant information, creates applicant journeys, records education
and test information, manages documents and files, tracks offers, maintains
checklists, schedules appointments, responds to notifications, and records the
final outcome of the applicant journey.

A Lead Manager normally works only with the leads, applicants, and records
assigned or made visible to them by an Admin.

Applicant

The applicant is the central subject of the system but is not a direct system
actor in V1. Staff users manage the applicant's information and lifecycle on the
applicant's behalf.

Authorized signatories may exist as document records, but they are not a third
actor type unless they separately hold an Admin or Lead Manager account.


Core domains (apps)
-------------------

- authentication — Owns login, account access, session identity, password
  management, account status, and enforcement of the Admin and Lead Manager
  actor types.

- leads — Owns enquiries before applicant conversion, including source,
  interests, assignment, stage, priority, follow-up, duplicate handling, loss
  reasons, and conversion.

- applicants — Owns the applicant's central personal and identity profile,
  including contact details, addresses, nationality, passport details, family
  information, emergency contacts, photograph, and applicant status.

- education — Owns academic history, qualifications, institutions attended,
  study periods, grades, GPA, percentages, backlogs, awarding bodies, education
  gaps, training, and related academic information.

- test_scores — Owns language and standardized test attempts, overall and
  section scores, dates, references, validity, expiry, and linked result files.

- applicant_journeys — Owns each applicant's specific overseas study objective,
  including target country, study level, field, preferred program, institutions,
  intake, budget, funding expectation, scholarship preference, stage,
  deferment, closure, and final outcome.

- institutions — Owns the institution and program catalogue, including
  countries, institutions, campuses, programs, qualification levels, fields,
  duration, intakes, tuition information, entry expectations, scholarships,
  dates, notes, and availability.

- offers — Owns institution offers connected to applicant journeys, including
  offer type, dates, conditions, tuition, scholarship, deposit information,
  acceptance, rejection, withdrawal, deferment, and final offer status.

- clients - Keeps basic records of the B2B clients. Basically a contact directory to manage relations.
| The data includes client company name, spokesperson, contact, logo, email, address,
| website and basic information only. This module is basic and for record keeping purpose. 

- documents — Owns editable document records, applicant or standalone
  ownership, document type, label, status, template association, and the saved
  input data used by the document workspace.

- document_history — Owns immutable print snapshots, print events, historical
  document versions, reprint history, generated-file references where used,
  and the identity of the user who produced each snapshot.

- document_templates — Owns document template definitions, categories,
  versions, availability, signatory records, signature images, signatory roles,
  and active or inactive status.

- uploaded_files — Owns files received from or connected to applicants,
  education records, journeys, offers, and documents, including category,
  ownership, version, verification state, replacement, rejection, archival,
  and restoration history.

- checklists — Owns the country requirement lists and each applicant's copy of
  one. An Admin authors, per destination country, the documents to collect and
  the stages to reach; when an applicant's journey names that country, the list
  is inherited automatically and tracked individually from then on. Holds
  required and optional items, item type, ownership, due dates, evidence
  references, and completion state.


- dashboards — Owns summarized operational views and reporting across lead
  funnels, sources, conversions, applicant volumes, journey stages, offers,
  pending documents, appointments, deadlines, Lead Manager workload, and final
  outcomes.

- notifications — Owns user-facing alerts for assignment, follow-up, missing
  information, missing documents, appointment dates, checklist deadlines,
  offer expiry, passport or test expiry, and important lifecycle changes.

- audit — Owns the immutable activity and change history for all important
  actions across Grandway, including actor, scope, action, time, reason,
  authority, source, previous value, and new value.

- search — Owns the one global search box: a single query answered across every
  searchable record type, grouped by type, handing off to each owning app's own
  list screen for the full set. Like dashboards, it owns no tables and holds no
  business rules of its own — it composes the search selectors of the apps that
  own the rows, and never widens what any of them would show the caller.


Cross-cutting principles
------------------------

Single source of truth

Each important real-world subject should have one authoritative record. The
applicant profile represents the person. Applicant journeys represent separate
study plans over time. Documents, files, offers, and activities should connect
to those records instead of duplicating identity information without a clear
reason.

Two-actor simplicity

V1 recognizes only Admin and Lead Manager as system actor types. Operational
responsibilities must be expressed through ownership, assignment, and access
rules rather than by introducing additional staff roles.

Admin authority with full accountability

Admins may perform any necessary action, but important Admin actions must never
be invisible. Full access does not remove traceability.

Assigned operational ownership

Every active lead and applicant should have a clearly identifiable responsible
Lead Manager unless deliberately left unassigned by an Admin.

Preserved history

Grandway should preserve the sequence of important changes instead of only the
latest state. Status changes, ownership changes, conversions, document prints,
file replacements, offer updates, archival, restoration, and final outcomes
must remain understandable later.

Traceability

Important events should capture, where applicable:

- Actor — who performed the action
- Scope — which lead, applicant, journey, document, or other record was
  affected
- Action — what occurred
- Time — when it occurred
- Reason — why it occurred, when explanation is required
- Authority — whether the action was performed as an Admin or Lead Manager
- Source — where the action originated
- Change — previous and new values where meaningful

Applicant-centred data ownership

The applicant owns their permanent personal and academic identity within the
domain model. Journeys, offers, documents, files, appointments, and checklists
should reference the applicant or the relevant applicant journey rather than
creating disconnected copies.

Journey separation

An applicant is not the same thing as a study journey. One person may pursue
multiple countries, programs, institutions, or intakes over time. Each journey
must retain its own state and outcome.

One destination at a time

A lead may be interested in several countries. An applicant is not: once a
person converts, they are working towards one destination, and the office
prepares accordingly. If they change their mind about the country, the current
profile is closed and they register again rather than the existing journey being
repointed. This is what makes country-driven behaviour — the document checklist
above all — unambiguous for any live applicant.

Document data and document presentation are distinct

Grandway stores document identity, ownership, status, template association, and
the entered data needed to prepare a document. The document workspace is
responsible for presenting the final document and calculating display-only
values where required.

For document types that contain derived values, such as bank statements or bank
certificates, Grandway stores the entered inputs and preserves print snapshots.
Derived presentation values are not treated as ordinary editable source data.

Immutable print history

A print snapshot represents what was used at a specific point in time. Later
changes to the applicant, document data, or template must not silently rewrite
historical print records.

Privacy by default

Applicant identity, passport, academic, financial, and supporting documents are
sensitive. Access should follow the actor's authority and assigned scope. Broad
access, exports, file replacement, and sensitive actions must be reviewable.

Explicit lifecycle states

Lead stage, applicant status, journey stage, document status, offer status,
checklist state, and appointment state are separate concepts. One status must
not be used as a shortcut for the whole lifecycle.

Archive before destructive loss

Where business history matters, records should normally be archived or
deactivated rather than silently removed. Any destructive action available to
an Admin must be deliberate and auditable.

Local operating context with future localization

V1 is designed for an education consultancy operating in the Nepal context and
working with foreign-study destinations. The conceptual model should not make
Nepal-specific assumptions that prevent future localization. English may be
the initial working language, while document and interface localization can be
expanded later.

Configuration should support operation, not hide the domain

The project may allow configurable statuses, templates, checklist definitions,
document categories, institutions, and programs, but configuration must not
make core domain meaning ambiguous.


Glossary
--------

Admin

A system actor with full access across Grandway. Admin actions remain subject
to audit and accountability.

Lead Manager

The operational system actor responsible for managing assigned leads and
applicants through the V1 lifecycle.

Lead

A person who has shown interest in foreign education but has not yet been
converted into an applicant.

Lead source

The origin through which a lead entered the consultancy, such as a referral,
walk-in, campaign, event, website, or other channel.

Lead stage

The current position of a lead before conversion, loss, disqualification,
duplication, or closure.

Applicant

The permanent person record created when a lead is converted or when an Admin
creates an applicant directly under an approved process.

Applicant profile

The applicant's central personal, identity, contact, family, passport, and
background information.

Applicant journey

A specific overseas study objective pursued by an applicant. It contains its
own target country, study level, field, institutions, programs, intake,
preferences, progress, and final outcome.

Journey owner

The Lead Manager currently responsible for the applicant journey.

Journey stage

The current state of an applicant journey, independent from lead, offer,
document, or appointment status.

Final outcome

The recorded end result of an applicant journey, such as successful, deferred,
withdrawn, rejected, lost, not qualified, cancelled, or otherwise closed.

Institution

An education provider that offers programs relevant to applicant journeys.

Campus

A specific location or branch of an institution.

Program

A course of study offered by an institution or campus for a particular
qualification level and field.

Intake

The planned admission period in which the applicant expects to begin a program.

Offer

A formal admission-related outcome issued by an institution for a particular
program, campus, and intake.

Offer condition

A requirement that must be fulfilled before an offer becomes complete or
unconditional.

Document

A saved editable document record containing document identity, ownership,
type, status, template association, and input data.

Document type

The recognized category or template family of a document.

Standalone document

A document that is not directly owned by an applicant. Its ownership and
purpose must still be explicit.

Document template

The approved presentation definition used to prepare a particular document
type.

Signatory

A stored person and signature record that may be used in approved document
templates. A signatory is not automatically a system actor.

Print snapshot

An immutable historical copy of the document information and relevant
configuration captured when the document is printed or formally saved to
history.

Uploaded file

A file received from or associated with an applicant, journey, education
record, offer, or document.

File version

A preserved revision of an uploaded file. Replacing a file should not silently
erase the earlier version when history is required.

Checklist template

A destination country's requirement list, authored once by an Admin. One active
template per country is the default — the list that country's applicants
inherit.

Checklist

One applicant's own copy of a country's requirement list, attached to their
journey and tracked independently of every other applicant's. Created
automatically when the journey's destination country is set, or applied by staff
by hand.

Checklist item

One individually trackable requirement within a checklist — a document to
collect, a stage to reach, or a task to perform.

Appointment

A scheduled activity connected to a lead, applicant, or journey.

Notification

An alert presented to an Admin or Lead Manager because attention or action is
required.

Ownership

The assignment of responsibility for a lead, applicant, journey, or related
work to a Lead Manager.

Assignment

The action through which an Admin or authorized process gives responsibility
for a record to a Lead Manager.

Transfer

A recorded movement of ownership from one Lead Manager to another.

Archive

A reversible state that removes a record from normal active work without
erasing its history.

Audit event

An immutable record of an important action or change, including the responsible
actor and contextual details needed for accountability.


Constraints / Out of scope
--------------------------

Hard constraints

- V1 has exactly two system actor types: Admin and Lead Manager.
- Admins can perform any action, but important actions must be logged.
- Lead Managers operate within the record scope granted to them.
- Applicants are not direct system users in V1.
- Every important lead and applicant action must remain attributable to a user.
- Historical document print snapshots must not be silently altered.
- Document records store editable input data; display-only derived values are
  not treated as ordinary persisted source data.
- One applicant may have multiple applicant journeys.
- Lead status, journey status, offer status, document status, checklist status,
  and appointment status must remain distinct.
- Each core domain must receive its own concept file before implementation.
- This document describes the project vision and scope; it does not authorize
  implementation by itself.

Out of scope for V1

- Additional system actor types beyond Admin and Lead Manager
- Applicant self-service portal
- Employment and work-experience management as a standalone domain
- Dedicated counselling-history or communication-history management
- Program shortlisting as a standalone domain
- Detailed institution application processing
- Visa case management
- Task management as a standalone domain
- Financial sponsor management as a standalone domain
- Consultancy invoicing, payment, accounting, or refund management
- Pre-departure management
- Email, SMS, chat, or messaging integration as a complete communication module
- Global search backed by its own search infrastructure — a search index, a
  separate search engine, semantic or vector search, or cross-type relevance
  ranking. (The `search` app under "Core domains" is not this: it owns no store
  and no ranking of its own, and only composes the trigram matching each owning
  app already implements. Scope revised 2026-08-02; the original line read
  "Global search as an independent domain" and was written against a search
  *store*, not a composition layer.)
- Import and export management as an independent domain
- Native mobile applications
- Government or institution decision-making
- Guaranteed eligibility, admission, or visa assessment
- Full university student-information functionality
- Automatic building of any module without its own approved concept document


Open questions
--------------

- What exact lead stages will Grandway V1 use?
- What exact applicant journey stages and final outcomes will V1 use?
- Can Lead Managers view all records, or only records explicitly assigned to
  them?
- Can a Lead Manager reassign or transfer a lead or applicant, or is that always
  an Admin action?
- Can Lead Managers archive records, or can they only request Admin action?
- Which Admin actions require a mandatory reason?
- Which sensitive fields or files should be hidden from Lead Managers by
  default?
- Should the project support multiple consultancy companies in one deployment,
  or only one consultancy?
- Which countries, institutions, and program categories are required for the
  initial release?
- Will institution and program information be entered manually, maintained from
  an external source, or both?
- What is the final canonical set of document types and templates for V1?
- Should final generated PDF files be retained in addition to document data and
  print snapshots?
- Which uploaded file types and maximum sizes are permitted?
- What verification states are required for uploaded files?
- Which countries need their checklist template authored before the first
  operational release? (The checklist family itself is settled: one per
  destination country, authored by an Admin. Which destinations are covered on
  day one is a data question, not a design one.)
- Which events should create notifications automatically?
- Which reports are essential for the first operational release?
- What data-retention period applies to lost leads, withdrawn applicants,
  archived documents, and uploaded files?
- Which actions, if any, should be permanently irreversible?
- Is English the only V1 interface language, or is Nepali support required at
  launch?
