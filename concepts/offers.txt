## Purpose

The Offers module tracks formal admission decisions tied to an applicant
journey.

It records what an institution offered, for which program and intake, under
which conditions, and how the applicant responded. Grandway needs this as a
separate record because a journey can be in offer stage without the system
knowing the exact offer details, and an applicant may receive more than one
offer over time.

## Relationship to the applicant journey

An offer always belongs to one applicant journey.

The journey remains the broader study plan. It holds the person's objective,
progress, and final outcome. The offer is one concrete institutional response
within that journey. Journey stage can indicate that offers are being awaited or
reviewed, but the offer module is the source of truth for the actual decision
records.

Offer records should also connect back to the institution catalogue entry they
were based on when possible. That preserves the original reference point even if
the catalogue later changes tuition, intake, or availability.

## Offer information

A useful offer record should capture:

* The applicant journey it belongs to
* The institution, campus, and program
* The intake or term the offer applies to
* Offer type, such as conditional or unconditional
* Issue date and any response deadline
* Conditions attached to the offer
* Tuition, scholarship, and deposit information relevant to the decision
* Acceptance, rejection, withdrawal, or deferment details
* Current offer status
* Notes and supporting files

The offer should preserve what was true when the institution made the decision.
Later catalogue edits, applicant updates, or intake changes should not rewrite
the historical offer record.

## Offer lifecycle

An offer usually moves through these broad states:

1. Draft or recorded
2. Issued
3. Awaiting response
4. Accepted
5. Rejected
6. Withdrawn
7. Deferred
8. Expired or no longer usable, if the deadline passes

The exact status vocabulary can be refined later, but the module must support
the common operational questions:

* Has an offer been received?
* Is it still open?
* Has the applicant accepted it?
* Was it rejected or withdrawn?
* Was it deferred to a later intake?

An offer may be superseded by a later offer for the same journey. The older
record should remain visible in history.

## Offer conditions

Conditions are the requirements that must be satisfied for an offer to become
fully usable or unconditional.

Typical conditions may include:

* Final academic results
* English test results
* Document submission
* Deposit payment
* Interview or verification
* Passport or identity confirmation
* Other institution-specific requirements

Conditions should carry their own status so staff can see what is pending,
fulfilled, waived, or otherwise resolved.

## Key user flows

1. Record an offer: A Lead Manager opens an applicant journey -> creates an
   offer from the institution/program being pursued -> records issue date,
   intake, tuition, conditions, and response deadline -> the journey now has a
   concrete admission decision on file.
   - If the offer was entered manually, the system should still preserve enough
     reference detail to identify the institution and program later.
   - If the source catalogue record no longer exists or has been made inactive,
     the offer history must still remain readable.

2. Review and decide: A Lead Manager opens the offer detail -> checks the
   conditions, deposit, and deadline -> records acceptance, rejection,
   withdrawal, or deferment -> the offer status updates and the decision is
   logged.
   - If the applicant accepts, the record should keep any acceptance notes and
     date.
   - If the applicant rejects or withdraws, a reason should be captured where
     appropriate.

3. Manage conditions: A Lead Manager or Admin updates an offer condition ->
   marks it satisfied, waived, or still pending -> the offer can be reviewed as
   complete or incomplete at a glance.
   - If a condition is added or changed later, the previous state should remain
     traceable in history.

4. Track multiple offers: A journey receives another institution response ->
   create a second offer without deleting the first -> compare outcomes and
   keep the full decision trail for the journey.
   - The journey may end with one accepted offer, several rejected offers, or a
     deferred decision that is revisited later.

## UI screens & wireframe notes

Offer List

Shows offers across journeys, newest first. Useful filters include applicant,
journey stage, institution, program, intake, and offer status. Columns should
show applicant name, institution, program, intake, issue date, deadline, and
current status.

Journey Detail → Offers panel

The main operational entry point. Shows all offers for that journey, with a
clear primary action to add a new one. Each row should show the institution,
program, intake, status, and response deadline.

Offer Detail

Shows the full offer record: linked journey, catalogue reference, conditions,
tuition, scholarship, deposit, notes, files, and history. Primary actions are
record decision, edit offer details, and update conditions.

New / Edit Offer Form

Administrative form for entering the institutional decision. Should capture the
reference to the institution and program, the decision details, the response
deadline, and any condition list. It must be clear whether the offer was
created from the catalogue or entered as a manual historical record.

Decision Dialog

A focused dialog for accept, reject, withdraw, or defer. It should require the
reason or follow-up detail whenever the selected outcome needs explanation.

## Constraints / Out of scope

* No application submission workflow. Offers record the institution's decision;
  they do not manage how the application was filed.
* No visa case management. A visa-related consequence of accepting an offer is
  handled elsewhere.
* No payments or accounting. Deposit amounts can be recorded, but payment
  collection is not part of this module.
* No automated eligibility scoring. The module records offer facts and
  conditions, but it does not decide whether an applicant qualifies.
* No silent rewriting of historical offers when the catalogue changes.
* No deletion of offer history. Replaced or outdated offers should remain
  visible as part of the journey record.

## Open questions

Settled in Phase 1 (2026-07-24):

* **Multiple active offers per journey?** Yes — unconstrained. Comparing
  competing institutional responses is the point of keeping offers as separate
  records, so a journey may hold any number, any number of them open at once.
  One rule is enforced: **at most one accepted offer per journey at a time**.
  No constraint is keyed on intake, because intake is still free text.
* **Exact status vocabulary?** Seven, not the eight listed above.
  `draft`, `issued`, `accepted`, `rejected`, `withdrawn`, `deferred`,
  `expired`. "Awaiting response" was collapsed into `issued` — once an offer is
  issued the consultancy is by definition awaiting a response, and two statuses
  for one state drift apart as staff forget to advance the second.
* **Conditions: reusable checklist or dedicated sub-record?** A dedicated
  `OfferCondition` sub-record, owned by this module. Decided by the project's
  state rather than by preference: `checklists` is a named future domain with
  no code behind it, and building a generic checklist inside `offers` would
  pre-empt it. Worth reopening when `checklists` ships.
* **Separate expiry date and action?** No. `response_deadline` alone.
  `expired` is a status a person sets, never the system — nothing in this
  deployment runs on a schedule (`notifications` does not exist), so an
  automatic expiry would be a promise the deployment could not keep. A computed
  `is_response_overdue` surfaces lapsed offers instead.
* **Lead Manager or Admin authority?** Any Admin or Lead Manager may do
  everything, read and write alike; Superadmin is denied outright. An offer is
  journey-scoped work with a blast radius of one applicant, and the Lead Manager
  running the journey is the person who receives the letter and records the
  answer. This is deliberately *not* the `institutions` split, where writes are
  Admin-only because catalogue data is shared infrastructure.

Two decisions were made that the questions above did not anticipate:

* **A decision is final; there is no reopen action.** Unlike an applicant
  journey, which can be reopened, an offer records what an institution decided
  at a point in time. An institution that changes its position has issued a new
  offer, and recording it as one is what keeps the decision trail readable.
* **Recording an offer never moves the journey's stage.** The two lifecycles
  stay independent in both directions — an offer can also be recorded against a
  journey at any stage, including a closed one.

Still open:

* Should offer conditions migrate onto `checklists` when that module ships, or
  stay owned here?
* Where should an offer letter PDF live? Nothing can be attached to an offer
  today; this waits on `uploaded_files`.
* Should a journey's free-text destination be reconciled against the catalogue
  reference on its offers? Today both are stored and nothing compares them.
* Does the offer list need text search across the snapshot institution and
  program names? Today only the catalogue foreign keys can be filtered, which
  misses every manually recorded offer.
