# APPLICANT JOURNEYS — Grandway

## Purpose

An applicant journey is one overseas-study objective pursued by one applicant.

A person is not the same thing as a plan. Someone may try for a master's in
Australia, have it fall through, and try again for a diploma in Canada two years
later. Those are two journeys belonging to one applicant, each with its own
destination, its own intake, its own progress, and its own ending.

The journey is where all the per-objective information lives: where they want to
go, what they want to study, when they intend to start, what they can afford,
how far along they are, and how it ended.

## Relationship to the applicant

One applicant has many journeys. A journey always belongs to exactly one
applicant and is never transferred to another.

The applicant record holds identity — name, contact, passport, family. The
journey holds intent and progress. Neither duplicates the other. A journey never
stores the person's name or phone number; it references the applicant.

A journey's stage never changes the applicant's status, and archiving an
applicant never closes their journeys. The two lifecycles run independently, by
design.

## How a journey comes into existence

Lead conversion

When an Admin converts a lead, an initial journey is created alongside the
applicant, seeded from whatever preliminary study interest the Lead Manager
recorded during follow-up.

That seeded information is a starting point, not a commitment. It was gathered
during an enquiry and is expected to be incomplete or to change. The journey is
the authoritative record from the moment it exists; the lead's study interest is
frozen history.

Manual creation

An Admin or Lead Manager creates a journey for an existing applicant. This is
the normal path for a second or third objective, and for applicants who were
created directly with no lead.

Either way the journey records who created it and which path was used.

## Journey information

* Target country
* Target institution and program
* Intended study level
* Preferred field or program
* Preferred intake
* Approximate budget and currency
* Scholarship interest
* Current stage
* Notes

Institution and program are free text for now. Grandway has no institution
catalogue yet, so there is nothing to point at; when the institutions module
exists these become proper references and the free text is migrated into them.

## Journey stages

The journey stage should be selected manually from a dropdown, as lead stages
are. Grandway does not infer progress.

Recommended V1 stages:

1. Planning
2. Profile Building
3. Shortlisting
4. Applying
5. Offer Stage
6. Visa Stage
7. Completed
8. Closed
9. Deferred

Planning

The objective has been recorded and is being discussed. Requirements are still
being established.

Profile Building

Academic records, test scores, and supporting information are being gathered.

Shortlisting

Institutions and programs are being considered and narrowed down.

Applying

Applications have been prepared or submitted.

Offer Stage

At least one institution decision is in play — awaited, received, or being
weighed.

Visa Stage

The applicant is preparing or awaiting a visa decision. Grandway tracks that
this stage is happening; it does not manage the visa case itself.

Completed

The journey reached a successful conclusion. Terminal.

Closed

The journey ended without success. Terminal, and always carries a recorded
outcome explaining why.

Deferred

The journey is paused to a later intake. It is not finished and not abandoned.

Completed and Closed are reached only through the deliberate close action, and
Deferred only through the defer action. None of the three can be picked from the
stage dropdown, for the same reason a lead cannot be marked lost from a
dropdown: each demands information that a stage change alone does not capture.

## Final outcome

Every closed journey records why it ended.

Recommended outcomes:

* Successful
* Withdrawn
* Rejected
* Not qualified
* Cancelled
* Other

Closing with Successful sets the stage to Completed. Closing with any other
outcome sets the stage to Closed. This keeps "how did it end" a single recorded
fact rather than something inferred from which stage the journey stopped at.

When Other is selected, an explanation should be required.

## Deferment

Deferring a journey means the applicant intends to continue but not on the
current timeline — a missed intake, a delayed test result, a family reason.

Deferring should record:

* The intake being deferred to
* An optional reason
* Who deferred it and when

A deferred journey is resumed by reopening it, which returns it to an active
stage. Deferment is not an outcome and does not close anything.

## Reopening

A completed, closed, or deferred journey may be reopened to an active stage.

Reopening should record who did it and when, clear the closure or deferment
state, and preserve the complete previous history. Reopening a completed journey
does not undo the fact that it was completed — that remains in the history.

## History

Important journey actions should appear in a chronological history, including:

* Journey created, noting whether from a lead conversion or manually
* Journey information changed
* Stage changed
* Journey deferred
* Journey closed, with its outcome
* Journey reopened

History entries should not be silently rewritten or removed.

## Permissions

Admin and Lead Manager

Both may:

* Create a journey for any applicant
* View and edit any journey
* Change journey stage
* Defer a journey
* Close a journey with an outcome
* Reopen a completed, closed, or deferred journey
* Review journey history

Journeys are shared, exactly as applicants are. Once a person is a client of the
consultancy rather than one Lead Manager's prospect, several staff will
legitimately work on their objectives.

Superadmin

No access. The Superadmin manages Admin accounts and does not participate in
consultancy operations.

## No deletion

Journeys are never deleted. A journey that should not have existed is closed
with the Cancelled outcome. The record and its history remain.

## UI screens & wireframe notes

Journey List

Shows every journey across all applicants, newest first. Columns: applicant
name, target country, study level, intake, stage. Filters for applicant, stage,
and target country. Useful as an operational worklist — "everything at Offer
Stage" — rather than as a per-person view.

Applicant Detail → Journeys panel

The per-person view. Lists that applicant's journeys with target country,
intake, and stage, plus a "New journey" action. This is the primary entry point;
the standalone Journey List is the secondary one.

New Journey Form

Fields for destination, institution and program (free text), level, field,
intake, budget, and scholarship interest. Nothing is required beyond the
applicant, because a journey often begins as little more than "Australia,
sometime next year."

Journey Detail

Header shows the applicant name (linking to their file), target country, and
stage. Panels for the objective's details, the closure or deferment state when
present, and history. Actions: change stage, defer, close, reopen. The stage
dropdown must offer only the six active stages — Completed, Closed, and Deferred
are reached through their own buttons.

Close Journey dialog

Requires an outcome. Reveals a required explanation when Other is chosen. Warns
that closing is reversible but recorded.

Defer Journey dialog

Requires the intake being deferred to, with an optional reason.

## Constraints / Out of scope

* No institution or program catalogue — free text until the institutions module
  exists.
* No offer records. Offers are their own module; the journey only tracks that it
  has reached Offer Stage.
* No visa case management. The journey records that a visa stage is underway,
  nothing more.
* No documents, files, or checklists.
* No automatic stage progression from any other module's activity.
* No journey transfer between applicants.
* No deletion.
* No per-journey ownership or assignment. Journeys are shared, like applicants.

## Open questions

* Is Visa Stage appropriate in V1 given that visa case management is explicitly
  out of scope for the project? It may be better modelled as a stage that simply
  means "post-offer, pre-departure."
* Should a journey carry its own priority or target date, to drive a worklist
  ordering better than "newest first"?
* Can an applicant have two open journeys for the same country and intake, or
  should that be prevented? V1 allows it.
* Should closing the last open journey suggest changing the applicant's status to
  Dormant? V1 does nothing automatically, but staff may want the prompt.
* When the institutions module arrives, how is existing free-text institution
  and program data migrated — automatically matched, or reviewed by hand?
* Deferment records a target intake as free text. Once intakes are catalogued
  this should become a reference.
